Is AI a Bubble?

The wrong question

Price already embeds a demand path. The question isn't "bubble or not" —

is the demand the price assumes actually achievable?

We stress-test that expectation — we don't call the top.

Teza lunch talk · 2026-06-25 · Artem + Matvei. Numbers web-verified, model re-run today; audit in the Reasoning Log.

A bubble is price far above the underlying. AI's underlying is the strange part.

Every past bubble had a physical underlying — railroads, dark fiber, houses, tulips. You could point at the overpriced thing.

AI's underlying is demand — a forecast, not an asset. So "overpriced" just means the demand assumption is too rich. That's why 1999 chart-overlays miss.

Micron: the thesis, proven in real time

  • Trailing screamed bubble: ~49× — the bears' exhibit A.
  • Then the earnings arrived: HBM sold out through 2026, HBM4 ramping 2×.
  • Multiple collapsed to ~24× trailing, ~9× forward — earnings rose, price didn't fall.

A rich trailing multiple is a bubble only if the forward earnings never show. Micron's just did.

Public $-feeds on MU are scaled/unreliable — the load-bearing facts are the multiple (a ratio) and the sold-out HBM order book, not the dollars. And one supply-side quarter validates 2026–27; it doesn't resolve 2028 or enterprise ROI.

The edges rhyme too: Anguilla now funds ~47% of its budget off the .ai domain — 4% in 2018. The dotcom land-grab, again.

The demand is committed, not narrated

  • Hyperscaler capex: $226B → $410B → ~$700B ('26 guide) → >$1T ('27). ~75% AI.
  • That capex is the complex's revenue. NVDA DC: $194B (FY26) → ~$343B (FY27 cons.).
  • Goldman: ~$5.3T cumulative AI capex, 2025–30.

Demand isn't the question — it's already booked. The bet is duration: does end-AI ROI sustain the spend? Watch the capex guide, not the P/E.

The EPS bar — what demand does the price assume?

Bottom-up EPS vs street consensus. Funded through 2027, conditional in 2028 — irreplaceables (TSM, NVDA) defensible; high-overshoot names (CRDO, semicap, MRVL) need the aggressive case.

Demand evidence — and the trap

  • Adopter screens are noise. "AI-adopters outperform" — nothing across ~2,000 names. Goldman's AI basket beats the market, but that's capex-winners, not adopters.
  • The soft spot is enterprise ROI. MIT/NANDA 2025: ~95% of corporate gen-AI pilots show no measurable P&L.
  • Sell-side is structurally long. Only buy-side says "1999" (Acadian's Lamont); banks stay long while calling sentiment "euphoric."

Sell-side silence isn't the all-clear — nor are a few hedged warnings.

The froth is real — but it's positioning, not fundamentals

$464B levered-ETF exposure; a record $185B inflow in two months. The froth is in the positioning & flows — a flows-unwind risk, not the fundamentals.

…and it's leveraged retail, piled into the AI names

Single-stock levered ETFs barely existed in 2023 — now ~205 underliers, AUM piled into the leaders (MU $8B, NVDA $6B, TSLA $5B).
