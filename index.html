Is AI a Bubble?

The wrong question — and the one we can actually answer

The price already embeds a demand path. So don't ask "is it a bubble?" Ask:

Are the expectations the price is paying for actually achievable?

We don't predict the pop. We stress-test the expectations.

Teza lunch talk · 2026-06-25 · framing: Artem + Matvei. Every number web-verified — sources in the speaker guide.

A bubble is price ≫ underlying. AI's underlying is the strange part.

**Every past bubble had a physical underlying.** Railroads. Dark fiber. Houses. Tulips. You could point at the thing that was overvalued.

AI's underlying isn't a thing. It's demand. How much the world will pay to use AI. So "overpriced" really means: is the embedded demand assumption too rich? That's a forecast, not an asset.

The uncomfortable cell: on capex-intensity we're already at or above dotcom. The thing that's different from 1999: it's funded by real cash and committed orders, not a story. The risk is duration, not solvency.

The demand is committed, not narrated

  • Hyperscaler capex: $226B ('24) → $410B ('25) → ~$700B ('26, guided) → >$1T ('27). About 75% is AI-specific.
  • That capex is the complex's revenue. NVDA data-center: $194B (FY26, actual) → ~$343B consensus (FY27).
  • Goldman: ~$5.3T cumulative AI capex, 2025–2030. Amazon ~$200B, MSFT ~$190B, Alphabet ~$185B, Meta ~$135B in '26 alone.

The bet isn't whether demand exists — it's already booked. It's duration: does end-AI ROI sustain the spend? Watch the capex guide, not the P/E.

We don't trust sell-side out-years. So we built our own.

Token demand + disclosed capex → an AI-compute $ envelope → our company graph attributes it to each name → EPS in 3 cases → invert it: what token growth is required to justify today's price?

Consensus is demand-funded through 2027, conditional in 2028. Bull: ROI proves out, multiples are cheap. Base: "good enough," multiples fair. Bear: hyperscalers cut — and that's the bubble.

Two knobs decide it: token volume × $/token deflation. They move together (heavy reasoning tokens deflate slowly), so the bull case is internally coherent and the bear needs two contradictory things at once.

Demand evidence — and the trap

  • The lazy metric fails. "AI-adopters outperform" shows nothing on a naive screen (we ran it across ~2,000 names). Goldman's AI-leaders basket has crushed the market — but that's capex-winners, not adopters. Don't confuse the two.
  • The real soft spot is enterprise ROI. MIT (NANDA), 2025: ~95% of corporate gen-AI pilots show no measurable P&L return. Hyperscaler spend is real now; whether customers earn a return on it is the open question.
  • Sell-side is structurally long. The voices willing to say "1999" are buy-side (Acadian's Owen Lamont). Banks stay long even while calling sentiment "euphoric."

Silence from sell-side isn't the all-clear. Neither is a handful of hedged warnings.

Froth at the edges, real earnings at the core

Levered single-stock ETFs, Micron's concentration in the semi ETFs, and a Caribbean territory funding nearly half its budget off the .ai domain. The mania is real — at the edges. The core (NVDA, the hyperscalers) prints cash.

Verdict

Not a 1999 bubble. A leveraged bet on demand duration.

  • On forward numbers, base/bull demand justifies the semis; the platforms are merely fair.
  • The real risk is a demand air-pocket and timing — not the valuation itself.
  • Artem's version: nothing wrong with riding it, if you sell five minutes before it pops. The whole game is knowing when.

Watch demand duration — hyperscaler capex guidance, realized $/token — not the P/E.
