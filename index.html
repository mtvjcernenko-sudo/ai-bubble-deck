Is AI a Bubble?

A forward-multiple / supply-demand framework

Teza lunch talk — Thursday 2026-06-25. Audience: quants (assume they know multiples & supply-demand; be sharp, ~8 slides). Framing: Artem + Matvei. Thesis: not a 1999-style pure-narrative bubble — a leveraged bet on whether AI demand sustains. Every number web-verified 2026-06-25; reasoning + sources in the companion Reasoning Log & Sources.md.

Slide 1 — The wrong question

"Is AI a bubble?" is the wrong question. Price already reflects priced-in expectations. So the question isn't "is it a bubble?" — it's:

Are the expectations the price is paying for actually achievable?

We're not predicting a pop. We're stress-testing the expectations.

Slide 2 — What a bubble is — and why AI's "underlying" is different

Classic bubble = price ≫ fundamental value. Past bubbles had a physical underlying (railroads, fiber, housing).

AI's underlying isn't a physical asset — it's demand: how much the world will pay to use AI.

→ "Overpriced" really means: "is the embedded demand assumption too rich?"

**The honest catch: the compression is a semiconductor story, not a universal one.** The AI-capex beneficiaries (NVDA, AVGO, MU, TSM) carry a steep trailing→forward gap — you're paying up today for an earnings ramp that still has to arrive. The mega-cap platforms (GOOGL, AMZN, MSFT, META) have forward ≈ trailing — priced fair, not cheap. So "is it a bubble?" is really a question about the semis, where the forward multiple is the bet.

The whole question collapses to one thing: do the ramping revenues reach the implied numbers? — and that bet lives in the AI semis.

The uncomfortable cell: on capex-intensity we're already at/above dotcom (Morgan Stanley / Todd Castagno: 32% peak-2000 vs 34% in 2026). The difference that matters: today it's funded by real cash and committed orders, not a story. So → not a 1999-style pure-narrative bubble — but the spend is dotcom-scale; the risk is duration, not solvency.

Froth at the edges — history rhymes: the .ai gold rush. Anguilla earned ~$93M from .ai in 2025 = ~47% of its government revenue (up from $2.9M / 4% in 2018; the ramp tracks the post-ChatGPT land-grab). Real earnings at the core, a domain mania at the edge — exactly the dotcom echo.

Slide 5 — So: will revenue get there? → supply/demand of AI

The demand is REAL and COMMITTED — not a 1999 narrative. The customers are already spending:

  • Hyperscaler capex: ~$226B (2024) → ~$410B (2025) → ~$690–725B (2026, guided) → >$1T (2027, MS proj.). ~75% AI-specific (~$450–500B in '26). Goldman: ~$5.3T cumulative '25–'30. (2026 by name: Amazon ~$200B · MSFT ~$190B · Alphabet ~$180–190B · Meta ~$125–145B · Oracle ~$55B.)
  • That capex is the complex's revenue: NVDA data-center ~$194B (FY26, actual) → ~$343B consensus (FY27). Accelerator TAM ~$160B (2025) → ~$440B by 2030; Huang floats ~$1T data-center capex by '28.

Slide 6 — Demand evidence (and the trap)

  • Tempting metric: "AI-adopters outperform non-adopters." We tested the naive version — it shows nothing (a global adoption screen across ~2,000 names is a bad metric). Goldman's AI-leaders/beneficiaries basket has crushed the market — but that's a capex-winners basket (NVDA/TSM/Oracle/Vertiv…), not a clean adopter-vs-laggard pair. → lean on real capacity / bookings / capex, not adoption screens.
  • **The soft spot is enterprise ROI, not hyperscaler capex: MIT (NANDA) "GenAI Divide," 2025 — ~95% of corporate gen-AI pilots show no measurable P&L return.** Committed hyperscaler spend is real now; whether end-customers earn a return on it is the open question (= the bear's "ROI disappoints" leg).
  • The sell-side bias: consensus is structurally long — career risk to call a top; it only flips after it bursts.

Silence from sell-side is NOT the all-clear. The cautious voices that exist are mostly buy-side / independent (Acadian's Owen Lamont: "2026 is looking like 1999") or hedged-but-still-long (Wells Fargo's Ohsung Kwon called sentiment "euphoric" — then said "own AI, don't fight the tape"). Sell-side consensus stays long. Neither silence nor a few hedged warnings is safety.

Slide 7 — Conclusion

**Not a 1999 bubble — a leveraged bet on demand duration.**

  • On forward numbers, under base/bull demand, the semis' multiples are justified; the platforms are merely fair.
  • The real risk is a demand air-pocket + timing, not the valuation per se.
  • Practical (Artem): nothing wrong with riding it — if you sell five minutes before it pops. The whole problem is knowing when. So the watch-item is the demand signal (hyperscaler capex guidance, realized $/token), not the multiple.

Verdict: priced for a specific demand path. Reachable in base/bull; the bear case is the bubble — concentrated in the AI semis. Watch demand duration, not the P/E.
