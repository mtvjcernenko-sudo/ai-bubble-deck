Is AI a Bubble?

The wrong question — and the one we can actually answer

The price already embeds a demand path. So don't ask "is it a bubble." Ask:

Are the expectations the price is paying for actually achievable?

We don't predict the pop. We stress-test the expectations.

Teza lunch talk · 2026-06-25 · Artem + Matvei. Numbers web-verified, model re-run today; audit in the Reasoning Log.

A bubble is price far above the underlying. AI's underlying is the strange part.

**Every past bubble had a physical underlying.** Railroads. Dark fiber. Houses. Tulips. You could point at the thing that was overvalued.

AI's underlying is demand. How much the world will pay to use AI. So "overpriced" really means: is the embedded demand assumption too rich? That's a forecast, not an asset — which is exactly why "looks like 1999" chart overlays miss.

The uncomfortable cell: on capex-intensity we're already at or above dotcom. What's different from 1999 — it's funded by real cash and committed orders, not a story. The risk is duration, not solvency.

The edges rhyme too: Anguilla now funds ~47% of its budget off the .ai domain — 4% in 2018. The dotcom land-grab, again.

The demand is committed, not narrated

  • Hyperscaler capex: $226B → $410B → ~$700B guided ('26) → >$1T ('27). About 75% AI.
  • That capex is the complex's revenue. NVDA data-center: $194B (FY26 actual) → ~$343B consensus (FY27).
  • Goldman: ~$5.3T cumulative, 2025–2030.

The bet isn't whether demand exists — it's already booked. It's duration: does end-AI ROI sustain the spend? Watch the capex guide, not the P/E.

Token demand + disclosed capex → an AI-compute $ envelope → our company graph attributes it per name → EPS in three cases → invert it: how much demand does today's price already require?

Consensus is demand-funded through 2027, conditional in 2028. The irreplaceables (TSM, NVDA) sit lowest — most defensible. The high-overshoot names (CRDO, the semicap trio, MRVL) need the aggressive demand case and can't all hold share. That's the 2028 short side.

Two knobs: token volume × $/token deflation. They move together, so the bull case is coherent and the bear needs two contradictory things at once.

Demand evidence — and the trap

  • The lazy metric fails. "AI-adopters outperform" shows nothing on a naive screen — we ran it across ~2,000 names. Goldman's AI-leaders basket has crushed the market, but that's capex-winners, not adopters.
  • The real soft spot is enterprise ROI. MIT (NANDA), 2025: ~95% of corporate gen-AI pilots show no measurable P&L return.
  • Sell-side is structurally long. The voices willing to say "1999" are buy-side (Acadian's Owen Lamont). Banks stay long even while calling sentiment "euphoric."

Silence from sell-side isn't the all-clear. Neither is a handful of hedged warnings.

Verdict

Not a 1999 bubble. A leveraged bet on demand duration.

  • Forward numbers justify the semis under base/bull demand; the platforms are merely fair.
  • The real risk is a demand air-pocket and timing — not the valuation itself.
  • Artem's version: nothing wrong with riding it, if you sell five minutes before it pops. The whole game is knowing when.

Watch demand duration — hyperscaler capex guidance, realized $/token. Not the P/E.
