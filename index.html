Is AI a Bubble?

A forward-multiple / supply-demand framework

Teza lunch talk — Thursday 2026-06-25. Audience: quants (assume they know multiples & supply-demand; be sharp, ~7 slides). Source: Artem + Matvei framing, 2026-06-24. Thesis: it's not a 1999-style bubble — it's a leveraged bet on whether demand materializes.

Slide 1 — The wrong question

"Is AI a bubble?" is the wrong question. Price already reflects priced-in expectations. So the question isn't "is it a bubble?" — it's:

Are the expectations the price is paying for actually achievable?

We're not predicting a pop. We're stress-testing the expectations.

Slide 2 — What a bubble is — and why AI's "underlying" is different

Classic bubble = price ≫ fundamental value. Past bubbles had a physical underlying (railroads, fiber, housing).

AI's underlying isn't a physical asset — it's demand: how much the world will pay to use AI.

→ "Overpriced" really means: "is the embedded demand assumption too rich?"

Slide 4 — Calibration: vs the Dotcom bubble

Dotcom was worse:

  • Companies produced ~nothing — $100B market caps for owning a domain.
  • Today's leaders earn real cash and ship real product (NVDA, hyperscalers).

Echo, not repeat: the modern domain land-grab — the .ai gold rush. Anguilla earned ~$93M from .ai in 2025 = 47% of its government budget — up from $2.9M / 4% in 2018 (the ramp tracks the post-ChatGPT land-grab: $32M/21% '23 → $39M/23% '24 → $93M/47% '25). Froth at the edges; real earnings at the core. [verified web Jun-2026]

But be honest on the one metric where we're dotcom-scale: Morgan Stanley — capex/sales ~32% at the dotcom peak (2000) → ~34% projected in 2026. On capex-intensity we're at/above 1999. The difference that matters: today it's funded by real FCF and committed orders, not narrative. So → not a 1999-style pure-narrative bubble — but the spend is dotcom-scale; the risk is duration, not solvency. [verified web Jun-2026]

Slide 6 — Demand evidence (and the trap)

  • Tempting metric: "AI-adopters outperform non-adopters." We tested it — it shows nothing. Naive adoption screens are a bad metric. → use real capacity / bookings / sell-side demand data instead. [DATA: sell-side AI-demand / semicap data, e.g. wk of Jun 12 positive note]
  • **The soft spot is enterprise ROI, not hyperscaler capex: the cited MIT study — ~95% of corporate gen-AI pilots show no measurable return.** Committed hyperscaler spend is real now; whether end-customers earn a return on it is the open question (= the bear's "ROI disappoints" leg).
  • The sell-side bias: consensus is structurally long — career risk to call a top; it only flips after it bursts.

Silence from sell-side is NOT the all-clear. A few cautious calls now exist (Wells Fargo "euphoric"; "2026 is looking like 1999"; the MS dotcom-capex parallel) — but consensus stays long. Neither silence nor a handful of hedged warnings is safety. [verified web Jun-2026]

Slide 7 — Conclusion

Not a 1999 bubble — a leveraged bet on demand.

  • On forward numbers, under base/bull demand, the multiples are justified.
  • The real risk is a demand air-pocket + timing, not the valuation per se.
  • Practical: nothing wrong with riding it — but the watch-item is the demand signal, not the multiple.

Verdict: priced for a specific demand path. Reachable in base/bull; the bear case is the bubble. Watch demand, not the P/E.
